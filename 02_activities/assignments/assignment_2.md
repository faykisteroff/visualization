Bad Visualization: CNBC Tesla vs Netflix Stocks

Link to image (the second chart on page titled "Tech Stock Throwback?"): 
https://www.cnbc.com/2019/06/12/tesla-looks-like-netflix-did-in-2011and-it-may-see-a-similar-recovery.html

1. Explain (with reference to material covered up to date, along with readings and other scholarly sources, as needed) why you classified that visualization the way you did.

In this graph here, CNBC analysts had tried to show that despite the (then) recent downturn of the Tesla stock price over 6 months (from Jan - June 2019) that it would have a similar recovery to the Netflix stock (over the period of June 2011 to Nov 2013). The first big issue is that it looks like they attempted to overlap the two lines for each stock, but that the pattern/trends for each one do not actually fit very well together. The Tesla stock (orange) shows a lot more fluctuations and changes compared to the Netflix data (blue). It's also very difficult for someone viewing this chart to accurately determine whether the timepoints overlap, since we are looking at a 6 month timepoint for Tesla, compared to an almost 2.5 year timeline for Netflix. 

The y axis labels on either side are also very different for each stock. For Netflix, we go from 0-10-30-50, without equal spacing between tick values. On the other hand, Tesla does not show a 0 marker and goes right from 160 by increments of 100. This shows quite a bit of stretching and manipulation of the real data points in order to fit both stock price histories into one graph and directly overlap them. 

There is also some very heavy use of shading and background design that makes the chart distracting to read and draws the views eye to areas like the top right corner with the highlighted accent. This shading also results in some data for the lowest price point of the Tesla stock to directly overlap and cover some of the Netflix price data as well.

2. How could this data visualization have been improved?  

This graph could be edited to improve in several different ways to improve readability and ensure the data is presented more accurately. First, we could include the addition of an x axis with a standardized date range. Labelling the dates on the bottom will help prospective investors and readers be able to determine if the stock is actually following a similar pattern at a similar timepoint, or if there are big changes between the two (e.x., if the Tesla price drop happened within a 4 month period while Netflix happened over a 8 month period). 

We could also begin both y axis from point 0 and increase them in equal increments. This does not have to be the same values and ranges for both sides, but could be something like; Tesla 0-75-150-225-300-375-450, and Netflix 0-10-20-30-40-50-60. This would ensure that data is not squished together but instead more accurately represented.

In addition, we could simplify the background and remove the cluttered design elements from the graph to make it simpler to read. Removing elements like the shading behind each line would allow data from one stock to overlap the other without blocking out any data from the shadows.

Lastly, we could also annotate the graph by adding some key important indicators such as earnings reports for each company in order to see how some external factors play into the stock price changes over time.


Good Visualization: Amazon Stock Market Trends

Link:
https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/rohini.tembhurnikar/viz/AmazonDailyStocks_V2/Dashboard1

1. Explain (with reference to material covered up to date, along with readings and other scholarly sources, as needed) why you classified that visualization the way you did.

This visualization here is a represention of several different features relating to the Amazon stock price and changes over time (from Jan 2025 to Sept 2025). The visualization title is very clear and immediately lets the reader/investor know what type of information they are looking at.

The figures within the visualization are each clearly labeled and have appropriate x and y values and labels. The visualization also separates the different types of data points by putting the key daily values at the very top (open/close prices) and then including some more detailed charts lower down showing trends and patterns. This is similar to how an investor might want to read the data, first by looking at daily/short term values before broader trends. This indicates that this visualization was made with the intended audience in mind.

The visualizations also use appropriate colours and contrast to highlight the changes in price very clearly. Since red and green are conventional colours for stock price analysis, these are generally the best and clearest to use. Using a dark background is also beneficial for making sure that the lighter green is more easily legible.

Each chart within the visualization is also very clearly labelled with a title and appropriate tickers on both the x and y axis. The timelines for all of the individual charts are also uniform, so that it's easier for the reader to skim between each one visually to look at different aspects of the stock.


2. How could this data visualization have been improved?  

Similar to the previous "bad visualization" example, these charts could benefit from adding annotations for significant prices changes/fluctuations based on earnings reports or other environmental factors that could have been contributing factors. 

In addition, on the "Price Over Time" chart, there is a bar chart (conventionally trading volume) embedded in grey but without a label to describe what this is. This bar chart could also benefit from having a y axis label on the right hand side to indicate trading volume, especially since there is a volume indicator value on the top of the visualization.
